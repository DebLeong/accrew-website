The Hard Truth

What Most Professional Services
Firms Get Wrong With Finance

Client profitability

"We've been working with this client for three years. I have no idea if they're actually profitable"

Revenue and profitability are different numbers. Without project-level cost tracking — time, direct expenses, allocated overhead — you don't know which relationships are worth growing and which are costing you margin.

Work in progress

"Our WIP balance is huge but our cash position is thin. What's going on?"

Unbilled work, delayed invoicing, and clients who pay late all show up as a gap between your accrual P&L and your bank balance. Tracking WIP separately from billed receivables makes this visible before it becomes a cash problem.

Time billing alignment

"Our time-tracking system and our accounting system don't talk to each other"

Revenue recognised in Xero, hours logged in a separate system, invoices in a third — reconciling these each month is manual, error-prone, and gives you numbers that don't reconcile cleanly for audit or partners.

Partner performance

"We have three partners. I'd like to understand each one's book of business separately"

Departmental or partner-level P&L requires dimensional tracking in your accounting system — cost centres, class tracking, or job codes. Without it, you have one number for the whole firm instead of insight by team.

Fixed fee vs. T&M

"We moved to fixed fees to smooth revenue, but we don't know if they're profitable"

Fixed-fee engagements can be highly profitable or silently loss-making depending on actual hours spent. Tracking time against fixed-fee contracts is the only way to know when to reprice or renegotiate.

Compliance burden

"The regulatory filing deadlines for a Hong Kong firm caught us off guard the first year"

Profits tax, MPF, employer returns for every staff member, and an annual audit if you're a limited company. These are non-negotiable. Missing them costs money and regulatory goodwill.
